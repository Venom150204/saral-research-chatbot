For immediate release. Researchers have introduced BERT, a breakthrough machine learning system that transforms how computers understand human language. In artificial intelligence research, language models are computer programs designed to read, interpret, and generate text in ways that are useful for everyday applications like search engines and virtual assistants. Traditional language models process text strictly from left to right, which fundamentally limits the surrounding context they can examine for each word. BERT overcomes this limitation by introducing a pre-training approach for deep bidirectional representations, allowing the model to look at both left and right context simultaneously. This dual-direction design is achieved through unsupervised tasks, including masking fifteen percent of input tokens and predicting them from both directions, alongside training the model to understand sentence relationships. In rigorous benchmark testing, the large version of BERT achieved an 80.5% score on the GLUE benchmark, marking a 7.7 absolute improvement over previous methods. The system also reached a 93.2 F1 score on SQuAD 1.1 and an 83.1 F1 score on SQuAD 2.0, setting new state-of-the-art results across eleven natural language processing tasks. Industry experts note that this architecture successfully generalizes across diverse tasks, significantly reducing the need for heavy task-specific engineering.
