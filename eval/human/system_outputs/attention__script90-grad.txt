When studying sequence transduction models, graduate researchers often encounter a major computational bottleneck in recurrent neural networks, where sequential token processing prevents parallel training across time steps. To overcome this fundamental limitation, the Transformer architecture entirely replaces recurrence and convolutions with attention mechanisms, allowing much greater parallelization for sequence models and significantly faster training. Under the hood, the model adopts a standard encoder-decoder structure consisting of six identical layers for both the encoder and decoder stacks, creating a deep hierarchical feature extractor. Each of these layers features a feed-forward network combined with multi-head self-attention, which runs eight parallel attention layers to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces simultaneously. Because pure attention operations are inherently permutation invariant and lack a built-in notion of sequence order, sinusoidal positional encodings are injected into the input embeddings to convey token positions. Computationally, self-attention layers require constant sequential operations compared to O of n operations for recurrent layers, yielding significantly shorter path lengths between long-range dependencies. These methodological choices ultimately translate into robust model capacity, demonstrated by strong generalizability on downstream tasks like English constituency parsing.
