Hello, and welcome to today's technology briefing for members of the press. Researchers have just announced a major breakthrough in artificial intelligence that promises to fundamentally reshape how machines process and understand human language. For years, traditional sequence models relied on recurrent neural networks, processing words one after another in a sequential chain. This step-by-step approach created a major computational bottleneck, making it very difficult to take full advantage of modern parallel computing hardware. Now, a new architecture called the Transformer entirely replaces recurrence and convolutions with pure attention mechanisms, allowing much greater parallelization and significantly faster training for complex sequence tasks. In machine translation, evaluating output quality relies on standardized benchmark scores that measure how closely automated translations match professional human references, establishing an objective standard for progress in the field. In benchmark tests on the WMT 2014 translation tasks, the big Transformer model achieved twenty-eight point four BLEU on English-to-German, outperforming previous best results by over two points, and reached forty-one point eight BLEU on English-to-French. Training this large model required just three point five days on eight P 100 graphics processing units, demonstrating that attention-only architectures can deliver state-of-the-art performance with high efficiency.
