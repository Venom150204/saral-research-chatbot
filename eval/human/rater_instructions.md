# Rater instructions

Thank you for helping. You will read short scripts written for different audiences and
score each one. It takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

## What you receive

- `rater_pack.md`: numbered items. Each item shows the request, the source slides the
  script had to be based on, and the script.
- `rating_sheet_<your name>.csv`: one row per item for your scores.

You are not told how each script was produced. Please rate on your own and do not
compare notes with the other raters until everyone has finished.

## How to score

Give each script three scores from 1 to 5.

**Audience fit.** Is it written for the audience named in the request?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wrong audience: unexplained jargon for a general audience, or too shallow for experts |
| 3 | Mostly suitable, with clear lapses |
| 5 | Reads as if written for exactly this audience |

**Factuality.** Judge only against the source slides shown with the item, not against
what you know about the paper.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | States things that contradict the slides or are not in them |
| 3 | One or two small details the slides do not support |
| 5 | Everything said about the research is supported by the slides |

**Helpfulness.** Could someone use this script for its stated purpose?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Not usable |
| 3 | Usable after significant editing |
| 5 | Usable as it is |

In the comments column, quote any sentence you believe is not supported by the slides,
and note anything else that drove a low score. Comments are optional but useful.

## Returning your scores

Fill in every row, save the CSV with your name in the file name, and send it back.
Individual scores are reported as given; they are not averaged away.
